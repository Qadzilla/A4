INSTRUCTIONS FOR RECIPIENT

Basis. Your basis is generally the cost of the property, including commissions and fees. Increase it by reinvested dividends and by any loss disallowed under the wash-sale rule. Decrease it by returns of capital. Basis is seldom the same figure as the purchase price, and the adjusted basis is the only figure from which gain or loss may properly be computed.

Box 1e. If this box is blank, your broker did not report a basis. This does not mean your basis is zero. You must determine and report the correct basis. If you cannot substantiate it, the entire proceeds may be treated as gain.

Transferred securities. Where shares were received from another firm, adjusted basis may not have been furnished. The obligation to report the correct figure remains with you. Records of the original purchase, of subsequent reinvestments, and of any prior disallowed loss should be retained for as long as the position is held and for three years after its disposition.

Box 1g. A loss on securities repurchased within 30 days before or after the sale is disallowed. Add the disallowed amount to the basis of the replacement shares. The loss is not forfeited; it is recovered on the later disposition of the replacement shares.

Holding period. Securities held more than one year are long-term. A position held one year or less is short-term and taxed at ordinary rates. The holding period begins the day after the trade date of acquisition and ends on the trade date of the sale.

Incorrect basis. If the basis shown is incorrect, report the correct amount and enter adjustment code B. Enter the basis shown on this statement in column (e) of Form 8949, the correction in column (g), and the resulting gain or loss in column (h).

Identification of lots. Absent identification at the time of sale, the first shares acquired are treated as the first sold. Lots differ in basis and in holding period; a specific-lot instruction given to the broker at or before settlement is the only means of altering the order.

Reinvested dividends. Amounts reinvested in additional shares are added to basis in the year credited. A holder who fails to add them reports a smaller basis than he holds and pays tax twice upon the same money.

Box 2. The short-term and long-term boxes pertain to short-term gain or loss and long-term gain or loss. Where the term is shown as short-term and box 12 is not checked, both the basis and the term rest upon your own records.

Box 4. Shows backup withholding. Generally, a payer must backup withhold if you did not furnish your taxpayer identification number to the payer. Include this amount on your income tax return as tax withheld.

Box 12. If checked, the basis in box 1e has been reported to the Service. Where it is not checked, the figure shown, if any, is furnished to you only and has not been reported.

Boxes 14–16. Show state(s)/local income tax information. Keep for your records.

Retention. Keep this statement, together with your basis records, with your permanent papers. It is not filed with your return.
